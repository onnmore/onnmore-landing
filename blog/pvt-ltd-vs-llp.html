Private Limited vs LLP: Which One Should You Actually Register in West Bengal?

Updated September 2026 7 min read Reviewed by Onnmore's compliance team

If you're starting a business in West Bengal, this is usually the first real decision you'll face — and it's more consequential than most founders expect. Get it right and your paperwork stays simple for years. Get it wrong and you may end up converting structures later, which costs more time and money than just choosing carefully now.

Here's the short version: Private Limited Company is built for businesses that plan to raise outside funding, issue shares to employees, or scale fast. LLP is built for founders — often professionals, agencies, or small teams — who want limited liability without the compliance load of a full company. The rest of this guide breaks down exactly why.

When a Private Limited Company makes more sense

  • You're planning to raise money. Angel investors, VCs, and most incubators only invest in private limited companies — it's the structure their term sheets are written for.
  • You want to offer equity (ESOPs) to early employees to attract talent without paying top-of-market salaries from day one.
  • You need credibility with larger clients or banks. Many enterprise clients and lenders simply prefer dealing with a Pvt Ltd company over an LLP or proprietorship.
  • You plan to scale headcount and ownership significantly — the share-based structure makes adding co-founders or investors cleaner than restructuring an LLP later.

When an LLP makes more sense

  • You're a service business or professional practice — consultants, agencies, CA/CS firms, small studios — where you don't need outside investment.
  • You want limited liability without heavy compliance. LLPs skip mandatory statutory audits below a turnover threshold, and have fewer ongoing filings than a Pvt Ltd company.
  • You're cost-conscious in the early stage — both registration and annual maintenance tend to run lower for an LLP.
  • Ownership will stay with the founding partners rather than being diluted across investors or an ESOP pool.
Neither structure is "better" in general — it depends entirely on where you expect the business to go in the next 2–3 years. If you're unsure, it's worth a quick conversation before you register, since converting an LLP into a Pvt Ltd company later is possible but adds extra paperwork and cost you can avoid by choosing right the first time.

Cost and compliance, in plain terms

Both structures give you limited liability — your personal assets stay protected from business debts either way. The real difference shows up every year after registration:

A Private Limited Company must hold board meetings, file annual returns with the Registrar of Companies (ROC), and get its accounts audited every year regardless of how much revenue it made. This is the tradeoff for the credibility and fundraising access it gives you.

An LLP only needs a statutory audit once turnover or capital contribution crosses a set threshold, and its annual filings (Form 8 and Form 11) are comparatively simpler. This keeps ongoing costs lower for smaller, steady-state businesses.

Our honest recommendation

If there's any real chance you'll raise outside funding or bring on employees with equity in the next couple of years, register a Private Limited Company from the start — converting later is possible, but it's extra cost and paperwork you can skip. If you're a founder-led service business that plans to stay lean and self-funded, an LLP will save you money and compliance headaches every single year.

Frequently asked

Can I convert an LLP into a Private Limited Company later?

Yes, this is a well-established process under the Companies Act, though it involves additional filings, cost, and time — generally more than if you'd registered the right structure to begin with.

Which one is cheaper to maintain every year?

LLPs are typically cheaper to maintain, mainly because the mandatory annual audit for a Private Limited Company applies regardless of turnover, while an LLP's audit requirement only kicks in above a threshold.

Do both protect my personal assets the same way?

Yes — both structures offer limited liability, meaning your personal assets are generally protected from business debts and legal claims, subject to the usual exceptions (fraud, personal guarantees, etc.).
